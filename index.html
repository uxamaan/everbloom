01 — The Stakes

01

The product was leaving a
market unclaimed.

72% of plant owners report their plants die within the first season — not from lack of interest, but from lack of timely, contextual guidance. Lotus Landscape, a Canadian gardening brand, had no digital touchpoint to retain customers after purchase.

Without one, they were losing ongoing engagement, monetisation potential, and any chance of building a loyal gardening community. The bigger opportunity wasn't just plant care — it was the $4.5 trillion global wellness industry intersecting with a generation using plants for stress relief, mindfulness, and mental grounding.

"EverBloom was designed to own that intersection — a seasonal garden companion combining AI-powered care, wellness, and community in one cohesive experience."

02 — The Research

02

Three sources.
Zero guessing.

Client Discovery Sessions

A structured Q&A with Lotus Landscape and PM Kashif Nazeer resolved 20+ product ambiguities before design began — covering onboarding depth, AI feature priority, content hosting, reminder complexity, journal privacy, and monetization model. Every major design decision traces back to a documented client answer.

Competitor Analysis — PictureThis, Planta, Greg

All three are plant databases with care reminders. None connects plants to mental wellness. None tell you that rubbing a eucalyptus leaf between your fingers is a grounding exercise, or that a sunflower's mood association is "energizing and uplifting." The competitive gap was not more plant data — it was a completely unclaimed wellness layer on top of every plant in the database.

User Story Documentation (33 Stories, 3 Tiers)

Product scoped into Seed (free), Garden, and Everbloom (premium) tiers with risks and assumptions documented per story. Key risk flagged early: notification fatigue — addressed through a general push notification with full detail on tap, not a flood of care-specific alerts.

Client AI Feature Priority

Garden Design → Plant Identification → Disease Detection → AI Chatbot. This ranking shaped which AI features were surfaced on the home screen — but the AI row itself was only added in a later iteration cycle.

03 — The Problem Statement

03

One sentence.
Four elements.

Hobby gardeners (wellness-focused, 25–45) fail to maintain consistent plant care routines because existing apps treat plants as database entries rather than living companions — offering generic care instructions with no seasonal context, no wellness connection, and no community to sustain the habit — resulting in high early abandonment and zero emotional attachment to the product.

✓ User Type ✓ Specific Failure Point ✓ Root Cause ✓ Measurable Behaviour

04 — Options Considered

04

What we considered.
What we rejected. Why.

Option A

AI-First Experience

Everything routed through one AI chatbot. Simple entry point, but maximum friction for a new user who doesn't know what to ask on day one.

✕ Rejected — chatbot ranked 4th in client priority
Option B

Dedicated Tab Per AI Feature

Plant ID, Disease, Garden Design — each with its own nav slot. Creates a feature catalogue. Free-tier users see locked tabs before experiencing any value.

✕ Rejected — navigation bloat kills first impressions
Option C

AI + Wellness as Contextual Layers

AI tools in a scannable row on Home. Wellness is embedded in every plant detail page. Discovery happens inside existing user journeys — no detour.

✓ Chosen — context was the value, not content volume

05 — Decisions + Tradeoffs

05

Every real decision
costs something.

1

Wellness as a per-plant layer, not a standalone section

Four dimensions per plant: Aromatherapy Benefits, Herbal/Medicinal Notes, Mindful Touchpoints, Mood + Energy Associations — embedded as horizontal scroll cards inside every plant detail page. A dedicated Wellness tab would give the content more visibility. We chose embedding because connecting wellness to a specific plant is more powerful than a generic content category. A user learning their sunflower is "energizing and uplifting" while looking at their own plant lands differently than a general article.

↑ Contextual relevance↑ Emotional attachment↓ Standalone feature visibility
2

AI Assistance row added to Home — post-initial design

The first version of the home screen had no AI section. After core flows were validated, a gap emerged: AI features had no discovery path for new users. Added in iteration two — placed below hero and Premium banner. Scroll hierarchy: welcome → monetization context → feature proof.

↑ Feature discoverability↓ Home screen density increased
3

Login animation changed from bottom-sheet to slide-up centred

Initial design had login as a bottom sheet — visually layered above the plant background. Engineering requested one system-wide animation pattern for consistency. Accepted a less dramatic visual treatment in exchange for a single consistent pattern across the entire app — eliminating implementation risk and timing inconsistencies.

↑ Dev consistency↑ Implementation reliability↓ Visual depth on login screen
4

Garden tab consolidates all plant features

All plant-related features (adding, care, journaling, reminders) live in one tab. Progressive disclosure handles the density — Journal and Reminders are accessed per-plant, not as a top-level dashboard, so new users aren't overwhelmed on first entry.

↑ Clear mental model↓ Tab is feature-dense on first entry
5

Accordion pattern for Advanced Plant Information

Plant detail screens contain 9 dense information categories. Dev lead Kashif raised whether an expandable accordion would work better than a flat list. Proposed, client approved, applied consistently. Hidden-on-load accepted because cognitive overload on first view was the larger risk for beginner-to-intermediate users.

↑ Reduced cognitive load↑ Visual consistency↓ Content hidden on load

08 — What's Next

08

Design is iterating.
Not done.

Test Wellness card position on Plant Detail

Hypothesis: moving Wellness cards above the Conditions grid makes wellness the first value signal — increasing time-on-screen and share rate.

Instrument onboarding drop-off

Instrumenting each onboarding step reveals whether the interest selection screen is where users abandon — and whether reducing options improves completion rate.

A/B test reminder notification format

General push vs plant-specific notification with inline action ("Water your Sunflower — tap to log"). Test after 30 days of user data.

Expand Mindful Touchpoints into a daily ritual flow

A "Today's Garden Ritual" card on home — pulling one touchpoint from the user's garden each morning — could become the app's highest-retention daily habit hook.
